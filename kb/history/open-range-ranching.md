# Barbed wire and the open range

Cheap wire fencing let ranchers and settlers enclose land at a cost they could afford. It helped bring the era of the open range and the long cattle drives to an end.

Homesteaders used barbed wire to protect their crops from roaming cattle, which put them in direct conflict with ranchers who depended on open grazing land.

In the early 1880s the "fence-cutting wars" broke out in Texas, as cattlemen who relied on the open range cut the fences of those who enclosed public land. Texas made fence cutting a felony in 1884.

Fencing also cut across the traditional routes of Native peoples and wildlife. Pronghorn, which usually pass under fences rather than over them, can be trapped by wire placed low to the ground.
