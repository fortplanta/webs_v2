# Barbed wire: invention

Before barbed wire, fencing the American prairie was expensive. Wood and stone were scarce on the plains, and living hedges such as Osage orange took years to grow, so most land stayed unfenced.

Early patents for barbed fencing were granted to Lucien B. Smith in 1867 and Michael Kelly in 1868, who called his design a "thorny fence". Neither became widely used.

In 1874 Joseph Glidden, a farmer in DeKalb, Illinois, patented a design in which a short barb was wound around a smooth wire and locked in place by a second twisted strand. It could be mass-produced and became the first commercially successful barbed wire.

Glidden went into business with Isaac Ellwood, and Washburn & Moen became a major manufacturer. Production grew from a few thousand pounds in 1874 to tens of millions of pounds by 1880.
