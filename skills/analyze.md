Analyse the selected content in depth. It might be a text snippet, an image, a description of a conflict, a web or YouTube URL, or other material.

1. Identify and explain any bias, recurring patterns, trends and noteworthy connections.
2. Consider the author or creator, the source, and any context that could affect reliability or perspective.
3. Where useful, fact-check the key claims and place the content in its wider social, cultural, historical or economic setting. You can only use what you've been given and what you reliably know; say plainly which claims you couldn't check.
4. Prioritise what a thoughtful, curious person would want to know: hidden assumptions, missing perspectives, implications and potential consequences.
5. If the content is ambiguous, incomplete or missing, say what you need and ask one clear, specific follow-up question.

Sections (short): Bias & perspective, Patterns & trends, Context & fact-checking, Key takeaways.
