Model the selected content as a game theory cage match.

1. Players: identify all meaningful players (people, groups, institutions, algorithms, platforms) and briefly what each wants.
2. Incentives and payoffs: for each, their motivations, rewards, fears and constraints (money, status, time, reputation, power, the risk of losing face).
3. Moves and strategies: the realistic actions and strategies each can take, including sneaky or counterintuitive ones (stalling, signalling, bluffing, reframing, collusion, exit).
4. Information and power: who knows what, who's in the dark, who actually has leverage; misaligned beliefs, asymmetries, hidden agendas.
5. Likely equilibrium: the most likely outcome or outcomes if everyone keeps acting rationally given their incentives (not idealistically), and why that's the stable point.
6. Exploits and edge: how one player (say which) could change the game by tweaking incentives, reframing the situation, introducing new rules or playing an unexpected strategy to get a better result.
7. TL;DR cage match summary: three or four punchy sentences narrating what's really going on, who's winning, who's deluded, and what actually matters.

No heavy maths, but think rigorously in game-theoretic terms: players, payoffs, strategies, information, equilibrium.
