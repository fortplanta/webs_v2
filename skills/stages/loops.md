Pick a path, but wire it with sensors and brakes.
- Select a primary option or portfolio. It must directly hit the bottleneck and respect hard constraints; favour high upside against downside, reversibility, and assumptions that can be tested quickly. Say why it wins.
- Design one to three feedback loops around it: the metric (what it really measures in terms of behaviour or outcome), the cadence, the threshold logic (continue, tweak or pivot), and the guardrails against over-optimisation side effects flagged in the Pattern Table.
- Define pivots and hedges: "if metric M does X by time T, switch to option B", or a hedge to run alongside the primary option.
