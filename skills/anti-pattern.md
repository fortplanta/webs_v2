Identify the recurring anti-patterns and failure modes in the selected content, which may be a text snippet, image, conversation, conflict, product, process or URL. Go beyond obvious beginner mistakes and surface the subtle, repeated screwups that experienced people still fall for.

For each anti-pattern you find:
1. Name it clearly and describe how it shows up in this specific input.
2. Explain why it's harmful, including likely short- and long-term consequences.
3. Design one or more practical safeguards that would actually work in messy real-world conditions (simple habits, structural constraints, default settings, social norms), not theoretical checklists.
4. Where relevant, give a concrete before-and-after example showing the input with and without that safeguard.

If an anti-pattern is deeply baked into the context (culture, incentives, platform, format) and not realistically fixable:
1. Call it out explicitly and explain why it's hard or impossible to remove.
2. Suggest how to minimise or dodge the fallout: workarounds, risk-limiting boundaries, early warning signs, how to opt out or contain the blast radius.

Structure: a short overview of the main anti-patterns, then a section for each with name, how it appears here, why it's bad, safeguards, and where needed an "unfixable but dodgeable" strategy.
