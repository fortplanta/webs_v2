name: Competitive landscape
mode: command
hint: Competitors, codes, whitespace and opportunity
---
Act as a senior strategist and analyst. Run a competitive and landscape analysis on the selected content, which may be a brief, a snippet, a URL (site, article, YouTube), an image, a product page, or a described conflict or situation.

First infer and restate the core analysis frame: the company or brand at the centre; the category and market space; the primary geography; the approximate timeframe; the audience, occasion or jobs-to-be-done (label these as inference if not explicit); and the priority channels. If anything is materially ambiguous, state your assumptions and proceed.

Scope: map direct, indirect and emerging competitors; decode category norms and codes; identify whitespace for distinct positioning, experience and creative direction.

For the focal brand and key competitors examine, where evidence is available and relevant: company basics; positioning (value proposition, reasons to believe, category entry points, pricing architecture); brand and voice; product or service (features, UX, differentiation, moats, switching costs, friction); go-to-market; creative and experience codes; demand capture and conversion; social proof and community; performance signals (growth proxies, not hard KPIs unless sourced); risks, constraints and regulations. Adapt depth to the input type (a YouTube ad: messaging, visual and sonic codes, implied positioning; a conflict: competitive dynamics, incentives, options; an image: visual codes and implied positioning).

Output in this order: 1) TL;DR in eight to ten sharp bullets. 2) The initial competitor set and your plan of attack. 3) A competitor table (positioning, audience, key benefits, pricing signal, key channels, standout creative and experience codes). 4) A cluster map in words of three to six clusters, with brands placed in them. 5) Category codes versus counter-codes. 6) A compact SWOT for the top three to five competitors. 7) A Porter's Five Forces summary, two to four lines per force. 8) Three to five opportunity territories, each with the core insight or tension, the strategic idea (what to own), a sharp positioning line and one creative or experiential starter. 9) An appendix of sources used and what each was used for, separating facts from interpretation.

Label claims [Fact], [Inference] or [Unknown]. Cite sources for any factual claim that isn't trivially obvious, preferring primary sources, and only cite what you were actually given or reliably know; acknowledge data gaps and never fabricate data or precise numbers. If critical inputs are missing, state assumptions and proceed rather than asking.
