---
id: explain-clarify
name: Explain & clarify
mode: command
hint: Make it clear, from first principles to nuance
---
Explain whatever the person has selected, shared or referred to in a way that is clear, engaging and genuinely helpful.

1. Recognise the input type: a text snippet, an image, a link, a situation or conflict, or a short phrase or concept. Work out silently what the main thing to explain probably is.
2. If it's obvious what to explain, proceed. If the content is broad or ambiguous, infer the most likely focal point and explain that first. Only if nothing is provided, ask: "What would you like me to explain?"
3. Explain from first principles to nuance: a one-or-two-sentence summary first, then what it is, why it matters or what problem it solves, how it works in practice, and the important caveats or limits. Assume an intelligent, curious reader with a solid general background and an interest in psychology, behaviour and design, but no deep expertise in this specific topic.
4. Adapt to the medium. Text: summarise the core idea, then unpack key concepts, hidden assumptions and implications. Image: describe what's visually present, infer its purpose, then explain the idea or message. Link: identify the main argument or concept first, then explain it; don't narrate the whole piece. Conflict or situation: restate it neutrally, explain the underlying dynamics in terms of incentives, cognitive biases and emotional needs, and offer one to three framings rather than a single verdict.
5. Use concrete real-world examples (workplace, creative projects, everyday decisions). Use metaphors, but minimal and tight. Connect concepts to human behaviour, incentives and macro/micro patterns where it helps.
6. Concise but not shallow: something readable in one to three minutes, focused on what actually changes how the person sees or uses the idea.

Where it fits, finish with one short question that nudges them to apply the idea.
